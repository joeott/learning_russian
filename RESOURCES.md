## 4. Pronunciation & stress tools (free — directly target your weak spot)

- **Forvo** — https://forvo.com/languages/ru/ 🆓
  *Why it fits:* Native-speaker recordings of individual words. Look up any hard dinner word (e.g. `прия́тного`, `здоро́вье`, `Миссу́ри`) and hear real stress + vowel reduction.
- **Wiktionary (Russian entries)** — https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Wiktionary:Russian_entry_guidelines 🆓
  *Why it fits:* **The "wikt stress" trick** — every Russian headword shows the stress mark (е́/о́/а́) and IPA. Search any word + "wiktionary" to instantly confirm which syllable is stressed before you say it aloud.

## How to spend limited time (tie resources to the schedule)

You're a busy lawyer with 16 days. Map resources to the moments you actually have:

- **Commute / cooking / dead time → passive input.** Queue **Russian With Max** or **Comprehensible Russian** (YouTube) and **Russian Made Easy** (podcast). This is the 10-minute fallback's "listen while brushing your teeth" — protect your ear daily even on zero-energy days.
- **Morning coffee, 5–10 min → Anki.** Audio-first reviews. This is non-negotiable on every day per the schedule.
- **Any hard word you'll need at the table → Forvo + the wikt-stress trick.** Before you trust a phrase, hear it on Forvo and confirm the stress on Wiktionary. Build a tiny "hard words" playlist (`прия́тного`, `здоро́вье`, `познако́мились`, `Миссу́ри`).
- **Active speaking days (Schedule Days 1, 4, 8, 9, 11) → AI role-play.** Use `tutor/roleplay_protocol.md` — this is where speaking fluency is built. Resources above feed it; the role-play makes it stick.
- **Days 7, 15, 16 → rehearse with Kadriya.** Validate the personal/flagged items (parents' name+patronymic, "Missouri", "I'm glad to be here") that no app or AI can verify, then bring them back into the role-play.
- **Spend ~60% on input/listening + AI speaking, ~30% on Anki, ~10% on Forvo/Wiktionary spot-checks.** Skip anything that's mostly reading drills — that's your strength, not your gap.

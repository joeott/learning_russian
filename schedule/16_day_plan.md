2. **5 minutes:** Listen to your web-app audio or podcast passively while commuting, cooking, or brushing your teeth (maintains *Comprehensible Input*). 
**Rule:** *No new material on 10-minute days. Just protect the foundation.*

### 📖 Detailed Daily Protocol

#### **PHASE 1: THE FIRST-CONTACT PROTOCOL (Days 1–2)**
*Goal: If you stop studying on Day 3, you can still confidently walk through the door, greet the parents appropriately, sit down, and raise a glass.*

**Day 1: Saturday, May 30 (2 hrs)**
*   **New Material Focus:** Formal greetings (*Здравствуйте*), Introduction (*Очень приятно*), Gratitude for hosting (*Спасибо за приглашение*).
*   **Review/SRS:** Set up Anki deck. Do 15 mins of initial card learning. **Ensure Anki is set to Audio-First** (listen to the Russian, then see the Cyrillic/English to train your ear).
*   **Input (Listening):** 30 mins web-app audio focused strictly on the rhythm and stress of these 3 phrases.
*   **Output (Speaking):** 30 mins AI Tutor role-play. Prompt the AI: *"Act as a Russian father-in-law opening the door. I will greet you."*
*   **Daily Success Check:** Can you say all three phrases smoothly from memory while maintaining eye contact with your wife?

**Day 2: Sunday, May 31 (1.5 hrs)**
*   **New Material Focus:** Survival table manners. "Very delicious!" (*Очень вкусно!*), and your first safe toast: "To our meeting/acquaintance!" (*За знакомство!*).
*   **Review/SRS:** 15 mins Anki (Day 1 + Day 2 vocab). You will notice Day 1 cards are easier (*Testing Effect*).
*   **Input:** 20 mins YouTube/web-app videos of Russian dinner tables/toasting to observe cultural body language.
*   **Output:** Rehearse the "Walk-in to First Drink" sequence with your wife. She plays her mom. Hand her a coat, sit down, wait for the pour, say the toast.
*   **Daily Success Check:** Did you successfully clink glasses and say *За знакомство* with correct stress on the 'о'?

### 🧠 Appendix: Scientific Principles Applied

Your schedule isn't random; it is engineered using established cognitive science frameworks to maximize retention for busy adults.

1. **Spaced Repetition System / SRS (Ebbinghaus, 1885; Mace, 1968):** You are using Anki daily. By reviewing vocabulary just at the edge of forgetting, you flatten the "forgetting curve" and push words into long-term memory efficiently.
2. **Retrieval Practice & The Testing Effect (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006):** Your schedule minimizes *re-reading* your cheat sheet and maximizes *recalling* information via web-app quizzes and AI role-play. Forcing the brain to retrieve the word builds stronger neural pathways than passive review.
3. **Comprehensible Input & Audio Primacy (Krashen, 1982):** Language acquisition requires understanding messages ("input"). Because your goal is oral, Days 3 and 5 force you to listen and comprehend before you are required to produce the language.
4. **Interleaving (Rohrer, 2012):** On Days 6 and 11, we mix up the topics (greetings, food, toasts) rather than practicing them in isolated blocks. This mimics real-life conversation and trains your brain to select the right vocabulary under unpredictable conditions.
5. **The Generation Effect (Slamecka & Graf, 1978):** On Day 7, you create your own "How we met" script. Research shows that material you generate yourself is remembered significantly better than material provided to you by a textbook.
6. **Desirable Difficulties (Bjork, 1994):** On Day 12, we introduce background noise. Making the training environment slightly harder than necessary ensures that the actual performance environment (a boisterous dinner table) feels easier by comparison.
7. **Sleep-Dependent Memory Consolidation (Stickgold, 2005):** The schedule tapers off new information and utilizes evening reviews followed by sleep. REM and slow-wave sleep physically reorganize the day's language input into procedural memory, turning awkward phrases into automatic responses.
